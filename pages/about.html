"Most people are not careless with money. They just never had the numbers in front of them clearly. That is the only difference between a good loan decision and a regrettable one."

When Rashid took his first loan, he did what most people do. He checked the EMI, found it affordable, and signed. What he did not see was the total interest he would pay over the full tenure, what even one part payment early on would have saved him, or what the real cost of carrying a credit card balance month to month actually was. That information existed. No one showed it clearly.

Over the years, working through personal loans, credit cards, and home loan decisions, the same pattern kept showing up. Good intentions, reasonable-sounding choices, and then a final number that felt much larger than expected. The cost of not knowing the full picture before signing is almost always more than people realise.

KnowYourEMI was built to change that. To put the complete picture in front of you before you decide, not after. Free, with no sign-ups, and with tools that go further than just telling you the monthly instalment.

His experience with loans, EMIs and credit is the starting point for the site's questions, while the calculators themselves are built from explicit mathematical formulas and stated assumptions.

How we work

How we build and check our calculators

A calculator is only useful when you can understand what it is calculating. We therefore try to make the inputs, formulas, assumptions and limitations visible instead of hiding them behind a result.

1. Formula based calculations

Loan tools use explicit mathematical formulas and month by month schedules where applicable. For reducing balance loans, interest is calculated from the outstanding principal rather than treating the original loan amount as the balance for every month.

2. Assumptions are stated

Calculator results depend on the inputs and assumptions shown on the tool. Lender policies, fees, rates, taxes and eligibility rules can differ, so a calculator result is an estimate for planning, not a lender approval or a promise of savings.

3. Primary sources for financial rules

When an article discusses regulation, credit reporting or consumer rights, we prefer primary sources such as the Reserve Bank of India and credit bureau documentation. Lender documents are used when the subject is lender specific.

4. We test changes before publishing

Calculator updates are checked against expected mathematical results and practical scenarios. When a calculation, explanation or assumption changes, we update the relevant page rather than quietly leaving an outdated explanation in place.

Editorial standards

What you can expect from our financial content

  • Clear separation between facts and estimates. Examples are identified as examples, and calculator outputs are not presented as guaranteed lender outcomes.
  • Source links where they matter. Regulatory and credit related claims are linked to the relevant primary or authoritative source whenever practical.
  • Plain language without hiding the math. We explain the number first, then show enough of the calculation or assumptions for a reader to understand where it came from.
  • Corrections are welcome. If you find a calculation error, broken assumption or outdated statement, email us at hello@knowyouremi.in with the page and the issue.
  • Financial education, not personalised advice. KnowYourEMI does not approve loans, set lender rates, provide investment recommendations or act as a financial advisor. The tools are intended to help you understand and compare numbers before making your own decision.
